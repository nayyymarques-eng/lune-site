An altar is a place in your home that exists only for what matters to you. It can be a whole shelf or a small tray on a window sill. You choose what goes on it, you tend it, and you come back to it. This guide covers what an altar means, where to place one, what to put on it, how to set it up step by step, and how to tend it through the moon's cycle.

## What a home altar is good for

- **A place to return to.** Most of the house is for doing things. The altar is for being still for a few minutes, and having a place makes the habit easier to keep.
- **Feeling connected to something bigger.** Many people feel there is more than what we can explain. An altar gives that feeling a place in your home, however you name it.
- **Remembering.** A photo, a letter or a small object can keep someone you love close.
- **Holding an intention.** Whatever you want to grow this month can live on the altar, where you see it every day.
- **Marking time.** Changing the altar with the moon or the seasons turns the year into something you notice, not something that slips by.

## What an altar means

People in many places and times have kept a small space at home set apart from daily use: a shelf, a niche in the wall, a corner of a room. The word itself comes from the Latin *altare*, a raised place. What these spaces share is not a list of objects but a gesture. You decide that this small place is different, and you treat it with care.

That is why there is no wrong altar. There are many ways to believe, and each one is whole. Your altar can hold a stone from a walk, your grandmother's ring and a bowl of water, or it can be a single flower in a glass. What makes it yours is that you chose it, and that you keep coming back.

## Where to place your altar

- **Where you pass every day.** An altar you see is an altar you tend. A bedroom shelf, a hallway table or a corner of your desk works better than a cupboard you rarely open.
- **Where you can pause.** Leave room to sit or stand in front of it for a few minutes without being in the way.
- **Near a window, if you can.** Daylight, and a view of the moon at night, make the space feel alive. Keep anything that fades, like photos and dried flowers, out of hard direct sun.
- **On a safe surface.** If you light candles, choose a stable, heat-safe surface, away from curtains, shelves above and anything that hangs. Keep it out of reach of children and pets.

Some people like to face the altar east, where the sun and moon rise. It is a lovely gesture, but the right place is the one you will use.

## What to put on your altar

Start small. Choose a few things from this list and let the rest arrive over time.

- **A cloth or a tray.** It marks the edges of the space. Natural fabric, a scarf you love or a wooden tray all work.
- **Something from the earth.** A stone, a crystal such as rose quartz, amethyst or citrine, a shell, a piece of wood, a pine cone, a little dish of soil or salt.
- **Something living or once living.** Fresh flowers, a small plant, a branch with leaves, dried lavender, rosemary or bay.
- **A light.** A candle, for indoors only, in a stable holder. If you cannot have a flame, a small lamp or a string of warm lights works.
- **A scent.** An incense you love, a resin like frankincense, a bundle of dried herbs, or a few drops of essential oil on a cotton pad. Burn anything indoors with a window open a little.
- **A bowl.** For water, [moon water](/journal/moon-water), rice, petals or salt. A bowl is the part of the altar you fill and empty, so it changes as you do.
- **Something for remembering.** A photo of someone you love, a letter, a small object that belonged to them.
- **Something for your intention.** A written sentence, a key, a seed, a feather, a ribbon in a colour that means something to you.
- **Something for you.** A journal and a pen, a deck of cards you like to read, a small mirror.

What to leave out: anything you keep only because you feel you should, anything sharp or unstable, and anything that belongs to a practice you are not part of. An altar is not a shop window. Fewer things, chosen well, hold more.

## How to set up your altar, step by step

### 1. Clear the space

Take everything off the surface. Dust it and wipe it with a damp cloth. This is the first gesture of the altar: you are making room.

### 2. Lay the cloth

Lay the cloth or set the tray down. Smooth it with your hands. The edges of the cloth are the edges of your altar.

### 3. Place the centre

Choose the one object that matters most right now and put it in the middle. It might be a candle, a stone, a photo or the bowl. Everything else will gather around it.

### 4. Add the rest, one at a time

Add each object slowly, and as you put it down, say in your head why it is there. *This stone is from the beach where I feel calm. This photo is for my mother. These flowers are for beginnings.* If you cannot say why an object is there, leave it out for now.

### 5. Arrive

Light the candle or the lamp, and the incense if you have one. Sit in front of the altar and take three slow breaths, longer on the out breath than the in breath. Look at what you made.

### 6. Set your first intention

Write one sentence on a small piece of paper that begins *"This moon, I want to…"*. Keep it about you and your own life. Read it aloud once, fold it and place it under the centre object.

### 7. Close

Thank the moon, or whatever you feel is bigger than you, and yourself for making the time. Put the candle out with a snuffer or a lid; never leave a flame burning when you leave the room.

## How to tend your altar with the moon

An altar is not finished when you set it up. It stays alive because you tend it. The moon gives a simple rhythm for that.

- **New moon.** Set a new intention. Write it, place it on the altar and add one object for it. The next new moon is on **Monday 9 November 2026**, in Scorpio. Our [new moon ritual](/journal/new-moon-ritual) and our [guide to the new moon in Scorpio](/journal/new-moon-in-scorpio-2026) pair well with it.
- **Waxing moon.** Each few days, refresh the water and the flowers. Read your intention again in your head.
- **Full moon.** Look back at what has grown. Make [moon water](/journal/moon-water) for the bowl, or follow our [full moon ritual](/journal/full-moon-ritual-for-beginners). The next full moon is on 24 November 2026, a supermoon in Gemini.
- **Waning moon.** Clear what is wilted, dusty or no longer meaningful. Give old water and petals to the soil. Our guide to the [waning crescent moon](/journal/waning-crescent-moon) has a practice for letting go.

A few minutes a day is enough. Light the candle, look at the altar, breathe, and put the candle out. The habit matters more than the length.

## Why a place like this helps

There is no study that measures what an altar means to a person, and Lune will never promise one changes your life. There is good research on rituals and on places. In a series of experiments, people who performed a short ritual before a stressful task felt less anxious and performed better than those who did not. Other experiments found that people who performed rituals after a loss felt less grief and more in control. A review of the psychology of rituals suggests they help people regulate emotion, focus on goals and feel connected to others. Research on attention also suggests that natural things, like plants, wood and stone, help a tired mind rest.

An altar brings these together in one humble place. It is a cue: every time you see it, your attention returns to what you chose to care for.
