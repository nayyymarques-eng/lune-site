The new moon is the quietest night of the month. There is nothing to see, which is exactly its meaning: the dark before a seed decides to grow. Across many calendars, the new moon marks a beginning. Months start with it, years start with it, and many people keep it as the night to name one thing and plant it small.

This guide walks through a new moon ritual gesture by gesture, with where each gesture comes from and why the way you name your beginning matters.

## What the new moon means

At the new moon, the moon passes between the Earth and the Sun. Its lit side faces away from us, and it crosses the sky together with the Sun, lost in daylight. For a night or two it is simply gone.

Then, low in the west just after sunset, a thin crescent appears and grows a little each evening for two weeks until the full moon. That is why the new moon reads as a beginning in so many places: it is the point from which the light returns.

## How people have kept the new moon

- **In the Islamic calendar**, each month begins when the first crescent after the new moon is seen in the evening sky. Ramadan begins and ends this way, and in many places people still go out to look for it together.
- **In China and much of East Asia**, the lunar new year begins on a new moon, usually the second after the winter solstice. Homes are cleaned before it, to make room for what comes.
- **In India**, the new moon is Amavasya. Diwali, the festival of lights, is kept on the new moon of the month of Kartika, the darkest night lit by thousands of small lamps.
- **In Buddhist countries**, the new moon is one of the uposatha days, kept more quietly and with renewed precepts.
- **In modern intention practice**, the new moon is when many people write what they want to begin, the gesture this guide is built around.

Different gestures, one habit: when the sky is darkest, prepare a clean space and begin.

## When to do your new moon ritual

The new moon is exact at one moment, listed to the minute in any moon calendar. Keep your ritual on the evening of that moment or on one of the two evenings after it. If you like the older way, wait for the first crescent: look west about half an hour after sunset, a day or two after the new moon, just above where the Sun went down.

## What you need

Nothing to buy. Choose a few things you already have that can stand for a beginning.

- **A page and a pen**, for naming what you begin.
- **Something to clean with**: a cloth for your surface, or a pinch of salt in a bowl of water to rinse your hands. Clearing before beginning is one of the most shared new moon gestures.
- **A seed of some kind**: a real seed or a bean you can plant in a pot, a bud, a small stone you will carry.
- **A cord or a ribbon**, to tie one knot for what you are beginning.
- **A light**, if you want one: a lamp or a candle on a steady plate, never left alone. At the new moon it is the only light on the altar, which is part of the meaning.

## A new moon ritual, step by step

### 1. Clear the space

Wipe the surface you will use, or rinse your hands in the salt water. Remove one thing from the space that does not belong there. Begin with room.

### 2. Arrive in the dark

Dim the lights. Sit, and breathe in through the nose for a count of four and out for a count of six. Longer out-breaths slow the heart a little. Stay until the day has stopped talking.

### 3. Notice what wants to begin

Ask yourself what wants to start this month. Not everything, one thing. It might be a habit, a conversation, a project or a way of being with someone. Let it come rather than choosing the most impressive one.

### 4. Name it, and its first step

Write it down as a beginning: "This month I begin..." Then, under it, write its very first step, small enough to take in the next seven days. Then write it once more as a plan: "When [a moment in your week], I will [the step]." For example: "When I make my first coffee on Monday, I will write one paragraph."

### 5. Tie it to something you can touch

Hold your seed, stone or cord while you read the step aloud. Plant the seed, put the stone in your pocket or tie one knot in the cord. The object will remind you when the page is out of sight.

### 6. Close

Fold the page and keep it somewhere you will find it at the full moon, about two weeks from now. Put out the light. On that night you will look back at what grew.

## New moon or full moon: which one?

Both, if you like. The two rituals are halves of one month:

| | New moon | Full moon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sky | Dark, moon not visible | Moon up all night |
| Meaning | Beginning, planting | Seeing, harvest |
| Gesture | Name one thing and its first step | Give thanks, release what has ended |
| The page | Folded and kept | Read back, and part released |

If you only keep one, choose the one that answers what your month needs: a start, or a clearing.

## Why naming one small step works

The moon does not make a plan come true. The way you write the plan matters.

- **Plans with a when and a what.** In a review of 94 studies, people who wrote their goal as "when this happens, I will do that" were far more likely to act on it than people who only named the goal (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006).
- **Fresh starts.** People are more likely to begin new habits at natural turning points like a new week, month or birthday, a pattern researchers call the fresh start effect (Dai, Milkman & Riis, 2014). The new moon is a turning point that comes back every month.
- **Rituals themselves.** A short ritual before a hard task lowered anxiety and heart rate and improved performance, and it helped most when people did the gestures as a ritual rather than as random movements (Brooks et al., 2016). Meaning is part of what works.
- **Writing it down.** Writing about what matters to you over a few days went with better health in the months after (Pennebaker & Beall, 1986). It was a small study, and writing can stir feelings at first. Go gently.

The new moon gives you a turning point twelve or thirteen times a year. The plan you write gives it somewhere to go.

## Making it yours

Some people keep one notebook for the whole year and write each new moon page in it. Some plant every seed in the same pot. Some only clear their desk and write one line. However you keep it, as a prayer, as a symbol or as a quiet evening, the beginning is wholly yours.
