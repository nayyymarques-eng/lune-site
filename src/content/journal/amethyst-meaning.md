Amethyst is the purple of the sky just after sunset, when the day is over and the night has not quite begun. It is the most loved variety of quartz, the birthstone of February, and one of the first stones many people place on a home altar. This guide covers what amethyst means, where it comes from, why its name means *not drunk*, how to care for it so its colour lasts, and a waning moon ritual with amethyst at its centre.

## What amethyst is good for

- **A focus for calm.** Its cool colour and weight in the hand make it a natural cue to slow down.
- **A clear head.** Its oldest meaning is sobriety and quick wits, which today reads as clarity: seeing a thing as it is.
- **Rest and the end of the day.** Many people keep amethyst beside the bed as part of an evening wind down.
- **Letting go.** It suits the waning moon, the nights when the light shrinks and you set down what you no longer need to carry.

None of this happens in the stone. Amethyst gives your intention a colour and a shape you can hold, and that is its whole gift.

## What amethyst is

Amethyst is quartz, the same mineral as clear rock crystal and yellow citrine. Its purple comes from traces of iron in the crystal, changed by natural radiation in the rock over a very long time. On the Mohs scale it is a 7, harder than glass, so it is durable enough to wear every day and to handle in a ritual for years.

Its colour runs from a pale lilac, sometimes called Rose de France, to a deep royal purple with flashes of red. The finest stones are an even, strong purple with no visible bands of colour. Amethyst often grows in geodes, hollow stones lined with crystals, and in Brazil and Uruguay some geodes are so large a person can stand inside them.

## Where amethyst comes from

People have worn amethyst for thousands of years. Beads of it appear in jewellery from ancient Egypt, and the stone was carved into seals and set in rings across the Greek and Roman world.

The name tells its oldest story. In ancient Greek, *amethystos* means *not drunk*. The stone's colour is close to that of red wine, and people linked it with Dionysus, the Greek god of wine, whom the Romans called Bacchus. From that link grew the belief that wearing amethyst, or drinking from a cup carved from it, kept a person sober. Other old beliefs held that it kept the wearer clear-headed in battle and in business. In the sixteenth century, the French poet Rémy Belleau gave the stone a myth of its own, a story of a young woman turned to crystal and stained purple with wine, and that tale is still the one most crystal books retell.

For most of history amethyst was a rare and costly gem, valued beside ruby and emerald and set in royal jewels, including the British coronation regalia. That changed in the nineteenth century, when huge deposits were found in Brazil. Today amethyst comes mostly from Brazil and Uruguay, with fine stones also from Zambia, Mexico and Bolivia. It is the birthstone of February and the gem of the sixth wedding anniversary.

## Amethyst and citrine: one stone, two colours

Amethyst and citrine are close kin. When amethyst is heated, its purple turns yellow, orange or reddish brown, and most citrine sold today began that way. In the Anahí mine in Bolivia, the two colours even grow in the same crystal, and the cut stone is called ametrine. If you keep both on your altar, you hold the two ends of the same story: the purple of dusk and the gold of morning. Our guide to [citrine](/journal/citrine-meaning) tells the yellow side.

## How to care for amethyst

- **Keep it out of strong sun.** Long exposure to bright sunlight can make some amethyst paler over time. A shelf away from a sunny window keeps its colour.
- **Avoid heat.** Strong heat turns amethyst yellow or orange, and sudden changes of temperature can crack quartz. No steam cleaning, no radiators, no hot water.
- **Clean it** with cool or lukewarm soapy water and a soft brush, then dry it with a cloth.
- **Store it apart** from softer stones like selenite or moonstone, which amethyst can scratch.

Many people like to cleanse a stone before a ritual. With amethyst, choose the gentle gestures: a rinse under cool running water, a night on the window sill under the moon (moonlight will not fade it), or a few moments in the smoke of an incense you love, indoors with a window open.

## When to work with amethyst

Amethyst fits the **waning moon**, the nights after the full moon while the lit part of the moon grows smaller each evening. In the moon's cycle, this is the time to set things down, rest and clear space before the next beginning. It matches amethyst's oldest meaning: a clear head.

This cycle's waning nights run until the new moon on **Monday 9 November 2026**, in Scorpio. Our guide to the [waning crescent moon](/journal/waning-crescent-moon) explains this last phase and its own rest rituals. The next waning stretch begins after the [full supermoon in Gemini](/journal/full-supermoon-in-gemini-2026) on 24 November.

Evenings suit amethyst too. Its colour belongs to dusk, and a short practice before bed is one of the simplest ways to keep it.

## Amethyst in astrology

Amethyst is the stone of February, the month of **Aquarius** and **Pisces**, and in modern astrology it is most closely tied to Pisces, the sign of dreams, feeling and the end of the zodiac's cycle. That fits the waning moon well: Pisces closes the zodiac the way the waning crescent closes the month.

You do not need to be a Pisces to keep amethyst. The more personal reading is where the moon falls in your own chart on the night you work with it. The [moon compass](/tonight) shows the house tonight's moon passes through for you, the part of your life the evening lights up, and that is a good place to point your ritual.

## What you need

- **An amethyst**, a tumbled stone, a point or a small piece of geode, any size you can close your hand around.
- **A purple, violet or white candle**, for indoors only, in a stable holder on a heat-safe surface.
- **A calming incense** such as lavender, sandalwood or frankincense, or a small bowl of dried lavender if you prefer no smoke.
- **A sprig of rosemary or a few dried lavender flowers**, laid beside the stone.
- **Paper and a pen.**
- **A small bowl of cool water** for the end of the ritual.

Arrange them in a small circle on your altar or a cleared table, the amethyst in the middle. If you are setting up an altar for the first time, our guide to [making a home altar](/journal/how-to-make-a-home-altar) walks through the space itself.

## A waning moon ritual with amethyst, step by step

### 1. Arrive

Dim the lights. Light the candle and the incense, indoors, with a window open a little. Sit where you can see the flame. Take three slow breaths, longer on the out breath than on the in breath. Let your jaw and shoulders soften.

### 2. Hold the stone

Pick up the amethyst and close your hand around it. Notice how cool it is and how slowly it warms. Turn it in the candlelight and look at where the purple is deepest. This is the moment of arriving fully: one quiet, cool thing in your hand.

### 3. Empty your mind onto the paper

On the paper, write down everything that is crowding your head tonight: the tasks, the worries, the conversations still running. Do not sort them. Write until nothing is left that wants to be written. Keep it about you and your own life.

### 4. Choose what to set down

Read the list once. Draw a line through everything you cannot act on tonight. Then circle the one thing you want to carry into the next cycle with a clear head. Under it, write one sentence that begins *"This moon, I set down…"* and finish it with what you are letting go of: the rush, an old worry, a habit that no longer helps you.

### 5. Seal it

Fold the paper so the crossed-out list is on the inside and lay the amethyst on top of it, with the rosemary or lavender beside them. Hold your hands over them for a few breaths and say the sentence aloud once, slowly.

### 6. Close

Dip your fingertips in the bowl of cool water and touch them to your closed eyes, as a way of saying the evening is complete. Thank the moon, and yourself for making the time. Put the candle out with a snuffer or a lid; never leave it burning.

Keep the amethyst on your altar or on the table by your bed until the new moon. On the night of the new moon, open the paper, read the one circled thing again, and let it become your intention for the cycle ahead. The crossed-out list can go in the recycling, folded and done.

## Why a gesture like this helps

There is no study showing that a stone changes your life, and Lune will never tell you otherwise. There is good research on what rituals and writing do. In a series of experiments, people who performed a short ritual before a stressful task felt less anxious and performed better than those who did not, and the effect depended on the gestures being framed as a ritual. A review of the psychology of rituals suggests they help people regulate emotion, focus on goals and feel connected. Writing also helps: in one sleep laboratory study, people who spent a few minutes writing a specific to-do list before bed fell asleep faster than people who wrote about tasks they had already finished. And in a brain imaging study, putting feelings into words calmed activity in the amygdala, the brain's alarm.

An amethyst on the bedside table works in a similar, humble way. It is a cue. Every time you see it, your attention returns to the choice you made under the waning moon.

## Find the moon in your own sky

The ritual works on any waning moon, but you can bring it closer. The [moon compass](/tonight) shows where tonight's moon falls in your own chart, and the [moon calendar](/moon) lists the next new and full moons in every sign.
