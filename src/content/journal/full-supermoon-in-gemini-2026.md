The full moon in Gemini is exact on **Tuesday 24 November 2026 at 9:54 AM ET** (14:54 UTC), at 2° Gemini. Look up on the evening of **Monday 23 or Tuesday 24 November**, whichever is clear. It is a supermoon, it is November's Beaver Moon, and it rises two nights before Thanksgiving in the United States.

Every full moon is a night of seeing: the light reaches every corner, the beautiful and the unfinished. This one shines in the sign of words, and it asks a simple question: what has been said this month, and what has not?

## What the full moon in Gemini means

In astrology, Gemini is a **mutable air sign** ruled by Mercury, the messenger. Air is thought and speech; mutable means quick, curious and able to change shape. Gemini is the sign of conversation, learning, siblings, neighbours and the short daily journeys that make up a life.

Every full moon sits across from the Sun. This one lights Gemini while the Sun is in Sagittarius, the sign of the long journey and the big question. The full moon pulls between the two: the near and the far, the small talk and the meaning, the many words and the one that matters.

So the full moon in Gemini reads as a night to:

- listen back over the month's conversations and notice what was really said,
- say the thing that was left unsaid, kindly,
- thank someone with words, out loud or in writing,
- let go of a story you keep repeating that is no longer true.

## The twins in the sky

The sign is named after the constellation Gemini, two parallel lines of stars headed by two bright ones, **Castor** and **Pollux**, the twins. On the night of this full moon they climb up in the east a few hours after the moon, two bright stars close together.

They are not true twins. Pollux, the brighter, is a single orange giant star. Castor, which looks like one white star, is a family of six stars orbiting one another.

In the Greek story, Castor and Pollux were brothers who shared everything. Pollux was immortal and Castor was not, and when Castor was lost, Pollux asked to share his immortality with him so they would never be apart. They were set in the sky side by side. Sailors called on them for safe passage, and in Roman times the twins were honoured in the Forum. In China the same two stars belong to the Well, one of the twenty-eight lunar lodges where the moon rests a night at a time.

Two stars that look alike and are not: a good picture for Gemini, and for any conversation where two people use the same words for different things.

## A supermoon, simply nearer

The moon's orbit is an oval. Once a month it swings closest to Earth, and when that happens near a full moon, the full moon looks a little larger and brighter. On 24 November the moon is about **360,754 km** away when it is full, and reaches its closest point the next day, at about 359,335 km.

The word "supermoon" was coined by the astrologer Richard Nolle in 1979, for a new or full moon within 90% of its closest approach. NASA notes that a full moon at its closest can look about 14% larger and 30% brighter than one at its farthest. Side by side the difference is clear; alone in the sky, most people notice the brightness more than the size. The full moon on **24 December** in Cancer will be nearer still, at about 356,700 km.

## Why it is called the Beaver Moon

The Old Farmer's Almanac popularised the Beaver Moon as the name for November's full moon, after the season when beavers finish their lodges and store food for winter. Other names for the same moon include the Frost Moon. The Chinese lunar calendar names its months by the season's work, and Japan keeps the autumn moon with tsukimi.

Every name says the same thing: this is the moon of finishing what needs finishing before winter. Coming two days before Thanksgiving, it is also a natural night to begin giving thanks early.

## A full moon ritual for the words that matter

You need a page and a pen, a card or a phone for one message, and a light: a candle or lamp on a steady plate, never left alone. Add anything that stands for speech to you, a feather, a bell, a book you love. Take your time.

### 1. Prepare

Find a window that faces east, or step outside after dark. The full moon will be rising, and later in the evening the twins follow it up the same side of the sky. Put your phone in another room until step 6.

### 2. Arrive

Breathe in through the nose, take a second short breath in on top, and let it all out slowly through the mouth. Repeat until your shoulders drop.

### 3. Look

Look at the moon for a while without doing anything else. Later, if you are still up, find Castor and Pollux, two bright stars close together, rising after it. Looking at something vast is part of the practice.

### 4. Listen back

Gemini's gesture: on the page, write the conversations of this month that stayed with you. For each, note what was said, and what was not. Do not judge them; just list them.

### 5. Keep and release

Circle the words you want to keep: things you were told, things you said and are glad of. Then cross out one story you keep telling, about yourself or someone else, that no longer feels true. Tear that line from the page and fold it away.

### 6. Say one thing

Thank one person, by name, for something specific they said or did this month. Say it out loud to the moon, then send it to them as a message or write it on a card. This is the one step that leaves the room.

### 7. Close

Look up once more. Put out the light. Keep the page with your circled words; they are worth reading at the next new moon, on the evening of 8 December in the Americas and 9 December in Europe.

## Why these gestures help

- **Looking at something vast.** Short walks that invited people to look for awe raised joy and warmth toward others over eight weeks (Sturm et al., 2022).
- **The breath.** Five minutes a day of cyclic sighing improved mood more than mindfulness meditation over a month (Balban et al., 2023).
- **Thanking someone in words.** People who wrote a letter of gratitude to someone underestimated how much it would mean to the person who received it, and how little awkward it would feel (Kumar & Epley, 2018).
- **Tearing up the old story.** People who wrote down a thought and threw the paper away relied on that thought less afterwards (Briñol et al., 2013).

The full moon gives the gesture a night. The words you choose to keep give it meaning.
